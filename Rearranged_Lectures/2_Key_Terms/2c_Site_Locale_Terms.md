- On-Premises: On-Premises (On-Prem) refers to software or hardware that is installed and runs on computers on the premises of the person or organization using the software or hardware, rather than at a remote facility such as a server farm or cloud.

- Cloud: Cloud computing is the delivery of computing services—including servers, storage, databases, networking, software, analytics, and intelligence—over the Internet (“the cloud”) to offer faster innovation, flexible resources, and economies of scale. You typically pay only for cloud services you use, helping you lower your operating costs, run your infrastructure more efficiently, and scale as your business needs change.

- Hybrid Cloud: A hybrid cloud is a computing environment that combines a on site and off site cloud. Specialized gateways and software are used to connect the two environments. This allows for data and applications to be shared between them securely.
  - Example: Organization keeps its workstations in the cloud and workers remote in for the work. Did this when working for the VA.
  - Example: Organiation keeps database on premises and web servers in the cloud to satisfy regulations for security and performance requirements.

- Public Cloud: A public cloud is a type of cloud computing in which a service provider makes resources available to the general public over the Internet. Public cloud services may be free or offered on a pay-per-usage model.

- Private Cloud: A private cloud is a type of cloud computing in which a service provider makes resources available to a single organization over the Internet. Private cloud services may be free or offered on a pay-per-usage model.

Government Cloud: A government cloud is a type of cloud computing in which a service provider makes resources available to government agencies over the Internet. Government cloud services may be free or offered on a pay-per-usage model.
